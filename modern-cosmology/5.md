## 5. The inhomogenous universe: matter &amp; radiation

### Boltzmann equation for photons

In order to develop a basic understanding of the cosmic microwave background,
we will first consider the behavior of photons and electrons near the era of recombination.
At that time, they are approximately in thermal equilibrium, 
with small inhomogeneities that we will treat with linear-order perturbation theory.

In thermal equilibrium without gravitational perturbations,
photons follow the Bose-Einstein distribution [(2.65)](#tag-2.65)
\\[
f^{(0)}(p, t)
= \f 1{ e^{p / T(t) } - 1 },
\tag{5.4}
\\]
which satisfies the Boltzmann equation for relativistic particles in the unperturbed FLRW metric [(3.39)](#tag-3.39),
\\[
\begin{aligned}
C^{(0)}[f^{(0)}]
&= \f{ \p f^{(0)} }{ \p t } - H p \f{\p f^{(0)}}{\p p}
\\\
&= -\brk{ \f{\dot T}{T} + H } p \f{\p f^{(0)}}{\p p}
= 0,
\tag{5.5}
\end{aligned}
\\]
with a vanishing collision term due to the equilibrium condition.

Near recombination,
photons are perturbed out of equilirbium due to Thomson and gravitational scattering with matter.
These kinds of scattering redirects photons but largely leave their energies unchanged,
so photon perturbations depend on $\uvec p$ but not $p$.

With a perturbation
$
\Theta(\vec x, \uvec p, t) \equiv \delta T(\vec x, \uvec p, t) / T(t)
$
to the mean temperature, $|\Theta| \ll 1$,
we introduce small inhomogeneities and anisotropies into the distribution.
To linear order in $\Theta$, they are
\\[
\delta f(\vec x, p, \uvec p, t)
\simeq \delta T \f{ \p f^{(0)} }{ \p T } 
= \Theta T \f{ \p f^{(0)} }{ \p T }
= - \Theta p \f{ \p f^{(0)} }{ \p p },
\tag{5.3}
\\]
where the last equality holds for any function of the ratio $p / T$.

The thermal perturbation $\Theta$ thus induces gravitational perturbations $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ of the same order.
The perturbed distribution $f = f^{(0)} + \delta f$ must then satisfy the general Boltzmann equation for relativistic particles in the perturbed FLRW metric,
\\[
\begin{aligned}
C[f] &=
(C^{(0)} + \delta C)[f^{(0)} + \delta f]
\\\
&\simeq
\zero{C^{(0)}[f^{(0)}]} + C^{(0)}[ \delta f ] + \delta C[ f^{(0)} ] + \zero{\delta C[\delta f]},
\end{aligned}
\\]
with the perturbation to the operator $\delta C$ given by [(3.74)](#tag-3.74).
In equilibrium, the $C^{(0)}[f^{(0)}]$ term vanishes.
To first-order in $\Theta$, 
we also ignore the $\delta C[\delta f]$ term,
so the Boltzmann equation reduces to
\\[
\begin{aligned}
C[f] &= C^{(0)}\brk{- \Theta
p \f{ \p f^{(0)} }{ \p p }
} + \delta C[f^{(0)}]
\\\
&= -\zero{ \Theta p\f{\p}{\p p} \prn{ C^{(0)}[f^{(0)}]} }
    - C^{(0)}[ \Theta p ] \f{\p f^{(0)}}{\p p}
    + \delta C[ f^{(0)} ]
\\\
&= -\brk{ \brk{ \f{\p}{\p t} + \f1a [\uvec p\cdot\nabla] + H }\Theta
    + \f1a [\uvec p\cdot\nabla]\Psi + \dot\Phi } p \f{\p f^{(0)}}{\p p}
\\\
&\simeq \brk{ 
    \dot\Phi
    + \f1a [\uvec p\cdot\nabla] (\Psi + \Theta) 
    + \dot\Theta 
} p \f{\p f^{(0)}}{\p p},
\tag{5.9}
\end{aligned}
\\]
where the final simplification is justified by [(5.5)](#tag-5.5),
assuming that the mean temperature $T$ varies slowly compared to its perturbation $\Theta T$ in time:
\\[
\f{\dot T}{T} + H
\simeq \dot\Theta + H
= 0.
\\]

Now perturbed from thermal equilibrium, the collision term no longer vanishes.
In the low-energy regime near recombination ($T \ll m\_e$), 
photons (momentum $\vec p$) predominantly interact through 
**Thomson scattering** with non-relativistic electrons (momentum $\vec p\_e$),
\\[\begin{aligned}
e^- + \gamma &\leftrightarrow e^- + \gamma,
\\\
\vec p\_e + \vec p &= \vec p\_e' + \vec p'.
\tag{5.10}
\end{aligned}\\]
The electron number density near recombination is also sufficiently small ($f\_e \ll 1$) for Pauli exclusion to be negligible.
The photon collision term according to [(3.48)](#tag-3.48) is thus
\\[\begin{aligned}
C[f(\vec p)] 
&= 
\f1{2E(p)}
\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2E\_e(p\_e)}
\int\f{\d^3 p'}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2E(p')}
\int\f{\d^3 p\_e'}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2E\_e(p\_e')}
\cdot |\mathcal M(\vec p,\vec p')|^2
\\\
&\quad\cdot [2\pi]^3\, \delta\_D^{(3)}(\vec p' + \vec p\_e' - \vec p - \vec p\_e)
\\\
&\quad\cdot 2\pi\, \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ E(p') + E\_e(p\_e') - E(p) - E\_e(p\_e) }
\\\
&\quad\cdot \Big[
f(\vec p')\, f\_e(\vec p\_e')\, [1 + f(\vec p)]
    - f(\vec p)\, f\_e(\vec p\_e) \, [1 + f(\vec p')]
\Big]
\\\
&= 
\f1{2E(p)}
\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2E\_e(p\_e)}
\int\f{\d^3 p'}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2E(p')}
\cdot \f1{2E\_e(p\_e')}
\cdot |\mathcal M(\vec p,\vec p')|^2
\tag{5.11}
\\\
&\quad\cdot 2\pi\, \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ E(p') + E\_e(p\_e') - E(p) - E\_e(p\_e) }
\\\
&\quad\cdot \Big[
f(\vec p')\, f\_e(\vec p\_e')\, [1 + f(\vec p)]
    - f(\vec p)\, f\_e(\vec p\_e) \, [1 + f(\vec p')]
\Big],
\end{aligned}\\] 
with the integral over $\vec p\_e'$ constraining $\vec p\_e' = \vec p\_e + \vec p - \vec p'$.
We will simplify this expression line-by-line.

The amplitude for Thomson scattering
can be derived from quantum electrodynamics to be
\\[
|\mathcal M(\vec p, \vec p')|^2 = 24\pi\sigma\_{\text T} m\_e^2 \brk{1 + \brk{\uvec p\cdot\uvec p'}^2}
\tag{5.17}
\\]
for randomly polarized photons,
where $\sigma\_{\text T}$ is the Thomson cross-section.
We average over $\uvec p$ and $\uvec p'$ to obtain
\\[
|\mathcal M|^2 = 32\pi\sigma\_{\text T} m\_e^2.
\tag{5.18}
\\]

Substituting in the dispersion relation $E(p) = p$ for photons
and $E\_e(p\_e) \simeq m\_e + [p\_e^2/2m\_e]$ for non-relativistic electrons,
the energy conservation constraint (to first-order in $\vec p\_e$) becomes
\\[\begin{aligned}
&\quad  \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ E(p') + E\_e(p\_e') - E(p) - E\_e(p\_e) }
\\\
&=  \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{
\brk{p' - p } +
\f{|\vec p\_e + \vec p - \vec p'|^2 - p\_e^2}
{2m\_e}
}
\\\
&=  \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{
\brk{p' - p} +
\f{\vec p\_e \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']}{m\_e}
}.
\end{aligned}\\]
In addition, because Thomson scattering is approximately elastic ($p\simeq p'$),
we can formally expand the Dirac delta as
\\[
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p} +
\f{\vec p\_e \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']}{m\_e}
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}.
\tag{5.15}
\\]

Furthermore, while the photon may scatter off in any direction ($\ang{\uvec p\cdot\uvec p'} \simeq 0$),
the electron momentum vector is only weakly perturbed: $\vec p\_e \simeq \vec p\_e'$.
Thus $f\_e(\vec p\_e) \simeq f\_e(\vec p\_e')$.

With all these approximations,
the collision term becomes
\\[\begin{aligned}
C[f(\vec p)]
&\simeq
\f1{2p}
\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2m\_e}
\int\f{\d^3 p'}{[2\pi]^3 \cdot 2p'}
\cdot \f1{2m\_e}
\cdot 32\pi\sigma\_{\text T} m\_e^2
\\\
&\quad\cdot 2\pi\brk{ 
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p} +
\f{\vec p\_e \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']}{m\_e}
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
}
\\\
&\quad\cdot [f\_e(\vec p\_e)]^2\, [
f(\vec p') - f(\vec p)
].
\\\
&\simeq
\f{4\pi^2\sigma\_{\text T}}{p}
\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3}
\int\f{\d^3 p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\\\
&\quad\cdot \brk{ 
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p} +
\f{\vec p\_e \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']}{m\_e}
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
}
\\\
&\quad\cdot f\_e(\vec p\_e)\, [
f(\vec p') - f(\vec p)
].
\tag{5.16}
\end{aligned}\\]
The $\vec p\_e$ integral terms are proportional to either
\\[
g\_e\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3}\, f\_e(\vec p\_e)
= n\_e
\quad\text{or}\quad
g\_e\int\f{\d^3 p\_e}{[2\pi]^3}\, \f{\vec p\_e}{m\_e}\, f\_e(\vec p\_e)
= n\_e \vec u\_e,
\\]
the electron number density and flux respectively.
Electrons have degeneracy $g\_e=2$,
and share their bulk velocity with baryons $\vec u\_e = \vec u\_\text{b}$,
thus
\\[\begin{aligned}
C[f(\vec p)]
&\simeq
\f{2\pi^2 n\_e\sigma\_{\text T}}{p}
\int\f{\d^3 p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\\\
&\quad\cdot \brk{ 
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p} +
\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']\,
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
}
\\\
&\quad\cdot [ f(\vec p') - f(\vec p) ].
\end{aligned}\\]

We evaluate this integral in spherical coordinates with solid angle $\d\Omega'$.
The distributional perturbation $\delta f$
and the scattering perturbation $|\vec p' - \vec p|$
are both independently small, 
so to first order in either kind,
\\[\begin{aligned}
C[f(\vec p)]
&\simeq
\f{2\pi^2 n\_e\sigma\_{\text T}}{p}
\int\f{\d p'\, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\int\d\Omega'\cdot
\Big[
\\\
&\quad
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot [ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
\\\
&\quad + \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot [ \delta f(\vec p') - \delta f(\vec p) ]
\\\
&\quad +
\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']\,
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot [ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
\\\
&\quad\Big].
\tag{5.19}
\end{aligned}\\]
The first term vanishes.
We evaluate the second term by writing $\delta f$ in terms of $\Theta$ via [(5.3)](#tag-5.3), 
so that
\\[\begin{aligned}
&\quad
\int\f{\d p'\, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\int\d\Omega'\,
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot [ \delta f(\vec p') - \delta f(\vec p) ]
\\\
&=
-\int\f{\d p'\, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\int\d\Omega'\,
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot \brk{
p' \,\Theta(\uvec p') \f{\p f^{(0)}(p') }{\p p'} -
p \,\Theta(\uvec p) \f{\p f^{(0)}(p) }{\p p}
}
\\\
&=
-4\pi\int\f{\d p'\, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot \brk{
p' \,\Theta\_0\, \f{\p f^{(0)}(p') }{\p p'} -
p \,\Theta(\uvec p) \f{\p f^{(0)}(p) }{\p p}
}
\\\
&=
-\f1{2\pi^2} \big[
\Theta\_0 - \Theta(\uvec p)
\big]
p^2 \,\f{\p f^{(0)}(p) }{\p p},
\end{aligned}\\]
where
\\[
\Theta\_0(\vec x,t)
\equiv 
\f1{4\pi} \int\d\Omega'\, \Theta(\uvec p', \vec x, t)
\tag{5.20}
\\]
is the temperature perturbation’s monopole moment.
The third term is first simplified by the angular integral,
then integrated by parts with the boundary term vanishing from the Dirac delta,
\\[\begin{aligned}
&\quad
\int\f{\d p'\, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\int\d\Omega'\,
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot
\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot [\vec p - \vec p']\,
[ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
\\\
&=
4\pi
\int\f{\d p' \, p'}{[2\pi]^3}
\f{\p}{\p p'} \delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot
[\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot \vec p]\,
[ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
\\\
&= 
-4\pi
\int\f{\d p'}{[2\pi]^3}\,
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot
\f{\p}{\p p'} 
\brk{
p'\,
[\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot \vec p]\,
[ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
}
\\\
&= 
-\f1{2\pi^2} 
[\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot \vec p]
\int\d p'\,
\delta\_D^{(1)}\prn{ p' - p}
\cdot
\brk{
p'
\f{\p}{\p p'} 
f^{(0)}(p') + [ f^{(0)}(p') - f^{(0)}(p) ]
}
\\\
&= 
-\f1{2\pi^2} 
[\vec u\_\text{b} \cdot \vec p]\,
p
\f{\p}{\p p} f^{(0)}(p).
\end{aligned}\\]
All combined, we find that
\\[\begin{aligned}
C[f(\vec p)] 
&\simeq -
n\_e \sigma\_{\text T} \brk{
\Theta\_0 - \Theta(\uvec p) + \uvec p\cdot\vec u\_\text{b}
}
p \f{\p}{\p p} f^{(0)}(p)
\tag{5.22}
\\\
&=
-n\_e \sigma\_{\text T} \brk{
\delta f(\uvec p) - \delta f\_0 + 
[\uvec p\cdot\vec u\_\text{b}]\,
p \f{\p}{\p p} f^{(0)}(p)
},
\end{aligned}\\]
where
\\[
\delta f\_0 \equiv -\Theta\_0\, p\f{\p}{\p p}f^{(0)}(p).
\\]
is the isotropic perturbation corresponding to the monopole moment $\Theta\_0$.

In the absence of bulk baryonic motion ($\vec u_\text{b} = 0$),
we have
\\[
C[f(\vec p)]
\equiv
\f{\d f}{\d t}
\simeq -
n\_e \sigma\_{\text T}
\brk{
\delta f(\uvec p) - \delta f\_0
},
\\]
and collisions drive the perturbation $\delta f(\uvec p, \vec x, t)$ to isotropy $\delta f\_0(\vec x, t)$
with characteristic timescale $n\_e \sigma\_{\text T}$.
However, when $\vec u\_\text{b}$ becomes significant,
anisotropies emerge in the photon distribution,
which produce imprints on the cosmic microwave background.

Equating [(5.22)](#tag-5.22) with [(5.9)](#tag-5.9),
we finally solve the Boltzmann equation for photons,
to first order in thermal and gravitational perturbations,
when scattering with low-density non-relativistic electrons:
\\[
    \dot\Phi
    + \f1a [\uvec p\cdot\nabla] (\Psi + \Theta) 
    + \dot\Theta 
    \simeq
n\_e \sigma\_T \brk{
\Theta\_0 - \Theta(\uvec p) + \uvec p\cdot\vec u\_\text{b}
}
\tag{5.23}
\\]
